Estate planning

A thoughtful plan can protect the people you love, reduce uncertainty, and make difficult transitions easier to manage.

Mr. D’Angelo assists clients with estate plans designed around their family, assets, and wishes. He also helps families carry out an existing plan after a loved one has died and guides clients through probate when no complete plan is in place.

Whether you are planning ahead or responding to a recent loss, the work should bring clarity and peace of mind—not another layer of confusion.

Standard estate plan

A standard estate plan addresses both end-of-life and post-death needs. At the center of many plans is a revocable living trust, which can provide a practical way to avoid a lengthy probate process while allowing the plan to change as family circumstances change.

A complete plan also helps the people responsible for carrying out your wishes understand what to do and reduces unnecessary obstacles for beneficiaries.

Since 2002, Mr. D’Angelo has helped clients prepare and revise living trusts for a wide range of family situations and estate complexities.

Already have a plan?

Estate plans should keep pace with changes in your family and your wishes. Existing plans can be reviewed and updated.

Conservatorships and special needs trusts

Conservatorships and special needs trusts can help protect people who are unable to care for themselves or manage their assets. A conservatorship may be appropriate for an aging family member who needs additional protection or for an adult with permanent disabilities through a limited conservatorship.

A special needs trust can allow a person receiving public assistance to use inherited or other assets for their benefit without unnecessarily jeopardizing that assistance.

These matters involve careful drafting, court oversight, and continuing accounting requirements. Mr. D’Angelo and his staff assist families from the initial application or creation through oversight and eventual termination.

Trust administration

When someone with a revocable living trust dies, the successor trustee must complete a detailed administration process. Mr. D’Angelo assists trustees whether or not he prepared the original trust.

The process commonly includes the notice required under California Probate Code section 16061.7, preparing trust accounting reports, gathering and selling assets, keeping beneficiaries informed, and preparing the trust for distribution.

Clear communication and an orderly process can help the trustee meet these responsibilities while keeping beneficiaries informed.

Probate process

When a person dies without an estate plan or without using an available probate-avoidance method, the estate may need to be handled through the probate court in the county where the person lived.

Depending on the assets and circumstances, a simplified procedure may be available. Mr. D’Angelo has experience with small-estate affidavits, petitions involving real property, spousal property petitions, and full probate matters.

He has handled probate filings in San Joaquin, Alameda, Contra Costa, Stanislaus, Amador, Lake, Madera, Sacramento, and San Mateo counties. That experience helps families avoid common procedural mistakes that can create additional delay and stress.
