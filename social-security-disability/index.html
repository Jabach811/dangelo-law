Two disability programs

The Social Security Administration administers two main programs that provide benefits based on disability.

Social Security Disability Insurance

The Social Security Disability Insurance program, commonly called SSDI or Title II, provides benefits to qualifying people who are insured through their work history and contributions to the Social Security system. Certain disabled dependents may also qualify.

Supplemental Security Income

The Supplemental Security Income program, commonly called SSI or Title XVI, provides payments to qualifying disabled adults and children who have limited income and resources.

The rules are specific to the program and the applicant.

Definition of disability

For adults applying under either program, federal law generally defines disability as the inability to engage in substantial gainful activity because of a medically determinable physical or mental impairment expected to result in death or lasting, or expected to last, for at least 12 continuous months.

The Social Security Administration’s definition may differ from the standards used by other government or private disability programs.

Mr. D’Angelo has handled claims involving both physical and mental impairments at multiple stages of the application process.

Preparing for a hearing

If a claim reaches the hearing stage, the applicant appears before an administrative law judge. This can feel daunting, especially while managing a serious health condition.

Mr. D’Angelo and his staff help clients prepare for the hearing, organize medical records, assemble written arguments, and understand what to expect. The aim is a clear, well-supported presentation of the disability and its effect on the client’s ability to work.
